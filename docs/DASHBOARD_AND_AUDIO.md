# Dashboard, navigation, sound and barrier recovery

The controls below work on Metro Loop and in the connected live traffic district. See LIVE_TRAFFIC.md for barrels, signals, traffic interaction and off-road recovery. Missions remain disabled.

| Control | Function |
|---|---|
| W / Up | Accelerate with ignition on and fuel available |
| S / Down | Brake; hold at standstill for 0.45 seconds to reverse |
| A/D or arrows | Steer; steering direction reverses while backing |
| Space | Momentary handbrake; briefly hold while steering at speed to initiate a controlled slide |
| U / Right Truck button | Right a rolled truck once it settles; keeps rollover damage |
| P | Parking brake latch; releases with P again |
| Q / E | Toggle left/right indicators; selecting one clears hazards |
| H | Toggle hazards; replaces individual indicators |
| L / K | Headlights / high beam (K also turns lights on) |
| I | Ignition on/off; an empty tank prevents starting |
| T | Set/cancel cruise above approximately 12 mph; braking, parking brake, ignition off or collision cancels it |
| B | Hold horn; Vehicle tab also offers a brief horn button |
| F | Demo refuel to full while stopped (not a delivery or service mission) |
| M / N | Expand GPS map / cycle local loop destinations |
| G / V | Recenter camera / toggle live rear-view mirror; GPS toggle is in HUD options |
| Tab | HUD/GPS, Vehicle and Audio controls; pauses simulation while open |
| Esc | Pause/resume; closes dashboard options first if open |
| C / R | Camera / safe-position reset (keeps fuel/ignition state) |

## Instruments

Speed is mph (`m/s × 2.2369363`), trip/navigation distance is miles. The tank holds 24 US gallons and starts with 18. Movement consumes fuel at a nominal 18 mpg, plus a small engine idle burn. Fuel and estimated remaining range respond to this model. Empty fuel cuts engine power; brake/coasting remain available. Stop, use F, then I to restart. Refueling is intentionally a demo convenience available anywhere while stopped.

Temperature warms toward 175–195°F depending on load and cools toward ambient with ignition off. Estimated RPM responds to speed, throttle and simplified automatic gear stages. This is not a physical drivetrain/cooling-system model; no engine damage, fuel quality, electrical-battery or ABS/TCS simulation is claimed. Warning text reflects implemented states: low fuel, engine off, parking brake, cruise, hazards and lights. Turn arrows and exterior indicators flash together; brake lamps respond to braking/parking brake.

The drawn needle/number layers remain live over the illustrated cabin. Headlights add a cel-style illumination pool on upward-facing world surfaces; high beam extends it from 35 m to 65 m. This lightweight shading effect does not model shadow occlusion or a real optical beam.

## GPS / HUD

Navigation uses the actual 260 m-radius road centerline: North Loop, City View, South Loop and Start / Service. Remaining distance follows forward travel along the ring; ETA uses current forward speed and shows `--` when stopped/reversing. Arrival is recognized within 16 m of a destination and remains latched until a new destination is chosen. These are fictional local waypoints, not online map data, physical exit ramps or scored missions.

M opens the route overview; N changes destinations. Tab's HUD/GPS page supplies clickable choices and visibility switches. Closing options or quitting saves HUD visibility and audio preferences to `user://driver_options.cfg`. It saves no personal data and makes no network calls. The mirror stops requesting render updates when switched off.

## Audio / atmosphere

Fourteen original mono 22,050 Hz 16-bit WAV files are generated by `tools/build_audio.py`. No external sound recording or paid service is required. Layered synthesized diesel-style idle/load follows RPM, throttle and camera; rolling noise follows speed; wind and periodic bird chirps supply daytime ambience. Horn, starter, relay ticks, brake-air release and impact thuds respond to controls/state. Tire squeal fades with sharp-turn lateral load and hard braking above low maneuvering speeds; ordinary gentle highway curves stay quiet. Foliage has a small shader sway. Baseline weather/time remains clear daytime.

Tab → Audio provides master level, nature/ambience level and mute. The sound design is a procedural first pass, not a recorded delivery-truck engine. Subjective timbre, speaker/headphone balance and long-term listening comfort need human audition. Suggested sequence: idle, accelerate through gear changes, coast, brake, toggle Q/E/H, hold B, switch I off/on, and compare cockpit/chase. Verify pausing stops playback and returning to the menu silences it. Quit drains active audio voices before engine teardown.

## Turning and vehicle motion

The truck retains lateral momentum as its heading changes instead of instantly redirecting all movement. Normal grip brings it back into line; Space reduces grip above 5 m/s while braking. Lateral speed is limited to 5 m/s and 35 percent of total speed. Release Space and ease/countersteer to recover. Parking and very low speeds clear lateral movement. This is an arcade handling model.

Steering now reaches full input in about 0.15 seconds, centers in about 0.13 seconds and reverses direction faster than the original setup. Turn authority is stronger at parking/city speeds and tapers as speed rises; reverse retains reversed steering. These defaults need human keyboard feel testing.

Hard braking, drift and sharp-turn tire stress leave four contact-based tire trails. Flat static surfaces are ray-tested beneath each wheel, so asphalt, plaza and grass work; airborne tires and moving vehicles do not receive floating marks. Trails remain for four gameplay seconds, then fade completely by seven. A fixed 2,048-segment MultiMesh pool bounds memory and uses one shared material. Fade timing pauses with the simulation, and resets/recovery break the trail connection rather than drawing a long teleport stripe.

C selects a chase camera with delayed heading, a soft shoulder offset during steering, and smooth position follow. Turns expose the truck's sides; the camera settles behind it afterward. Cockpit motion is deliberately subtle. The exterior body has damped roll/pitch and directional impact recoil, separate from the upright collision hull. Reset clears handling and suspension transients. Existing barriers exercise impact response; traffic and obstacle scenarios remain disabled.

## Barrier handling

### Rollovers and damage

A sufficiently severe contact enters physical crash simulation: at least 26 m/s total speed, at least 16 m/s inward, and either a significant lateral trip (inward speed times the sideways normal component >=12) or a frontal inward speed >=24 m/s. These are tuned demo thresholds. An oblique wheel trip supplies roll torque; frontal contact mainly pitches/recoils and can settle upright without counting a flip. Lower-speed and glancing contacts keep their bump/scrape behavior.

During a severe crash, a 3,200 kg RigidBody3D takes over the compound hull with a lower center of mass, gravity, contact friction and low bounce. The normal driving collider is disabled, and the exterior follows the body's actual pose. No fixed rotation curve or artificial airborne arc is used. Normal driving and the assisted wall climb still use the arcade CharacterBody controller; this is not a full wheel/suspension simulator.

Damage increments once per crash only when actual body tilt exceeds about 78 degrees, including landing on its side. Three such rollovers total the truck, lock out ignition/drive and emit hood smoke. U / Right Truck becomes available after motion settles, rather than after a fixed animation duration. Upright frontal crashes can recover automatically without spending a rollover. Recovery restores the pre-impact ignition state when fuel and damage allow; it does not repair damage. New Truck clears damage. Pause and menu resume preserve crash pose and momentum. Hood smoke still uses original fading world-space CPU puffs.

The previous long box could snag corners, lose steering as scalar speed fell to zero, or keep reporting speed into a wall. A horizontal capsule trial could climb low barriers. The final hull uses a central box plus rounded vertical cylinders at its ends: a stadium footprint with vertical sides. Impact removes the component of scalar speed directed into the wall; motion slides along it with a gentle solver separation. Contact at very low speed grants recovery steering when throttle or reverse is held. Reverse retains its steering convention. No position teleport or manual reset is needed to recover in the automated cases.

Regression tests drive into inner/outer barriers, then try both left/right steering in forward and reverse. Perform the same checks manually at shallow and head-on angles, including steering release and repeated glancing contact. R remains a general recovery option.

## Speed-sensitive contact and exhaust

Below 22 m/s (about 49 mph), impacts cause minor bump/recoil and speed loss without rollovers or permanent damage. Above that speed, a 4-12 m/s inward component produces a brief assisted wall climb with upward hull motion, body bank and scrape audio. Lift remains capped at 0.75 m; the truck drops back to the road. Heavier contacts use impact sounds/recoil and, when the severity criteria above are met, physical crash response. Very shallow scrapes do not launch the truck. These are tuned arcade thresholds, not real vehicle safety limits.

Collision entry selects original bump, metal scrape or heavy crash sounds and scales their level with inward impact speed. A short cooldown prevents contact chatter. A rear-left tailpipe releases small fading exhaust puffs on throttle acceleration, with brief repeat pulses under sustained acceleration. Idle, throttle release, engine-off, rollover and totalled states stop new exhaust. Existing emitted particles fade in world space. New-truck reset clears lingering smoke.
